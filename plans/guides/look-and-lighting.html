The Look & Lighting Guide

Everything that shapes how a Schooner scene looks — every material field, every light, fog, shadows, the post chain — what each parameter does, the value ranges that read well, and how to combine them for a given mood. Written for Kinesis (Game 1) against the forward renderer as it stands today.

Pillar fit: this is the engine “tailored, not generic.” The renderer is permanently Blinn–Phong + a warm filmic grade — no PBR, even in Game 5 — so the knobs below are the whole palette. Learn them and you own the look.

1 · Rendering philosophy

Three decisions colour everything else:

  • Blinn–Phong, not PBR. Surfaces have an albedo and a single roughness scalar that widens or tightens a specular lobe. There is no metallic, no GGX, no energy conservation. roughness here is an artistic dial on highlight width, not a physical microfacet term. This keeps the per-draw uniform tiny and the look hand-authored.
  • HDR linear in, filmic LDR out. The forward pass renders into a floating-point HDR target in linear light. Lights can push values well past 1.0. A post pass then runs a Narkowicz ACES tonemap to compress that HDR into displayable range with a soft highlight shoulder and gentle shadow toe — the “filmic” feel — before the color grade.
  • Mood is a post-process, geometry is a forward-process. Per-surface shading, lights, shadows, fog and god-rays happen in the forward shader. Zone mood (color cast, vignette, screen overlays) happens after, in the post chain, as cheap full-screen resources you can swap per scene or per gameplay beat without touching geometry.
The mental split you’ll use constantly: Forward answers “what is this surface and how is it lit here?”  ·  Post answers “what does the whole frame feel like?” Reach for a Material/Light knob for the first; reach for ColorGrade/Vignette/Overlay for the second.

2 · The frame pipeline

One frame, start to finish. Understanding this order is the key to predicting how two knobs combine — e.g. why fog dims an emissive lamp but the color grade can’t, or why god-rays survive a vignette.

Three consequences worth memorising.
  • Fog is pre-tonemap, grade is post-tonemap. Fog mixes in HDR linear against the lit color, so a glowing red lamp behind thick fog correctly dims to a red haze. The grade then shapes the already-foggy frame — your color cast is coherent with the fog, not fighting it.
  • God-rays are added after the fog mix but before tonemap. They’re extra radiance scattered into the view ray, so they can blow past white and get rolled off by the ACES shoulder like any other highlight.
  • Bloom is pre-tonemap too (step C0), so the glow rolls into the same ACES highlight shoulder rather than sitting flat on the LDR image — this is why it reads as soft 2005-era HDR halation, not a sticker. What blooms is decided by HDR brightness, not by which object it is: push a surface’s emissive or a light’s intensity past the threshold and it glows. See §13b.

3 · Linear vs sRGB — the one thing that silently ruins a look

Color textures (albedo) are uploaded sRGB (load_png); the GPU decodes them to linear automatically on sample. Data textures (normal maps) must be uploaded linear (load_png_linear) — they’re vectors, not colors, and an sRGB decode would bend them.

The asset layer already routes this for you: TextureAsset::is_data() returns true for normal maps, picking the linear path. When you add a new texture, the only question is: is this color or data? Get it wrong and normals come out subtly wrong (too weak, wrong direction) with no error.

Normal-map convention: the shader expects OpenGL (+Y-up green channel). The floor uses Metal021_..._NormalGL.png, not the DirectX _NormalDX sibling. If relief looks inverted (bumps read as dents under raking light), you’ve got a DX map.

4 · Material — the per-surface knob set

Material is an optional component. A mesh with no Material renders against Material::DEFAULT: white albedo, mid roughness, opaque, no texture. Spawn one to deviate. Every field, with the value that ships as default:

5 · Roughness → specular, in numbers

The shader maps roughness to a Blinn–Phong shininess exponent:

shininess = pow(2.0, mix(0.0, 10.0, 1.0 - roughness));
roughnessshininessReads as
0.05≈ 724Sharp pinpoint glint — wet glass, polished chrome, the eye’s cornea.
0.20≈ 256Clean metal panel, tight raking highlight (chamber walls).
0.35≈ 90Brushed/worn metal floor — a defined but spread highlight.
0.5032The default. Matches the Game-0 hardcoded baseline.
1.001Near-matte — concrete, painted drywall, the dark lab shell (albedo 0.02, roughness 1.0).

Two things to remember: the highlight color is always white (dielectric convention), and the matte specular floor is 0.3 — every surface has some glint unless Fresnel pushes it higher. There is no “turn off specular”; instead crank roughness toward 1 to spread it into invisibility.

6 · Textures, tiling & triplanar

UV mode (default). Mesh UVs × uv_scale + uv_offset. Use for authored meshes that ship their own UV layout (the eye glb). The sampler is Repeat, so any scale above 1 wraps and tiles.

6b · Triplanar projection

Set triplanar: true and the surface is textured by world-space projection: albedo and normal are sampled on the three world planes (X, Y, Z facing) and blended by the geometric normal. The payoff for Kinesis’ procedural architecture is huge:

  • The texture is continuous across separately-spawned boxes — no per-box UV seam.
  • No stretching on thin reveals (a wall’s edge doesn’t smear).
  • You never author UVs for room geometry — just spawn boxes.

In triplanar mode uv_scale.x is reinterpreted as world repeats per metre (y and offset ignored). The playground uses 1.0 / TILE_METERS with TILE_METERS = 2.0 — one texture tile every two metres, shared by every wall and floor.

Industry note. Normal maps naively triplanar-blended go flat; the shader uses Golus’s whiteout blend (2017) to reorient each plane’s tangent normal by the geometric normal before blending. You get correct relief on triplanar walls for free — just supply a normal_texture. Cost: 3× the texture samples of UV mode. Fine for indoor scale; revisit if outdoor geometry dominates.
Single-mip caveat. Textures are single-mip until Game 2A. Very high tiling (or thin distant surfaces) will shimmer — keep uv_scale / world-repeats modest.

6c · Normal maps & the “raked relief” look

A normal map perturbs the per-fragment normal so flat geometry catches light as if it had surface detail. The single most important authoring lesson here:

Keep normal_strength at 0.3–0.6. Full strength (1.0) reads as a high-frequency bump demo. The Schooner look is subtle relief raked by hard light — Half-Life 2 / Gothic, not a tech showcase. The playground holds walls and floor at 0.6, the eye at 0.5. The hero spot’s grazing angle does the rest: low relief + raking light = panelwork that reads as real.

Mechanically: the map’s tangent-space xy is scaled by normal_strength (0 = flat geometric normal, 1 = full authored relief), then rotated into world space through a TBN basis re-orthonormalised per fragment. FLAT_NORMAL binds when there’s no map, so the path is uniform.

7 · Glass & Fresnel

fresnel is the only knob that turns a translucent pane into believable glass. At 0 (default) every surface is a matte dielectric. Above 0, the shader applies Schlick’s (1 − n·v)⁵: the surface stays clear face-on but lifts both its specular highlight and its alpha toward 1 at grazing angles — reflective and opaque along the silhouette, exactly how real glass behaves.

The recipe is a combination, not a single field. From the playground window:

Material {
    albedo: Vec3::new(0.6, 0.7, 0.8),   // cool glass tint
    albedo_texture: Some(glass),
    roughness: 0.1,                     // smooth → tight reflections
    opacity: 0.2,                       // mostly see-through face-on
    blend: BlendMode::AlphaBlend,       // needs the transparent path
    fresnel: 1.0,                       // rim reflection ON
    ..Material::DEFAULT
}

fresnel pairs with low opacity + low roughness. High opacity defeats the see-through; high roughness blurs the rim glint that sells it.

8 · Decals & depth bias

A decal quad sitting flush on a wall z-fights — both surfaces are coplanar, the depth test can’t decide which wins, and you get shimmer. depth_bias (world metres) nudges the decal toward the camera along the view ray before the depth test: screen position is unchanged (it stays on the same ray), only its depth shrinks, so it wins depth_compare: Less without visibly floating off the wall. A few millimetres (0.002–0.01) is plenty. Shading still uses the true unbiased position, so lighting and shadows are unaffected.

9 · Lights

Three light types. All split unit-magnitude tint (color) from a scalar intensity so you can rebalance brightness without retouching hue. The rough per-draw contribution is:

albedo · color · intensity · attenuation · dot(N, L)

9a · DirectionalLight — the sun & the ambient carrier

Positionless (at infinity). Carries its own direction (the way light travels, sun→surface) — it does not pair with a Transform. The renderer uses the first one it finds.

FieldDefault (sun())Notes
direction(-0.4, -1.0, -0.3)Down-and-forward. Normalised in-shader.
color(1,1,1)Unit tint.
intensity1.0Set to 0 to keep only ambient (see below).
ambient(0.1, 0.1, 0.1)Flat fill added to every fragment, tinted by albedo. Stand-in for indirect light.
The ambient trick — use this constantly indoors. Ambient is read unconditionally from the directional slot, even when intensity = 0. So an indoor chamber with no sun still spawns a DirectionalLight purely to carry ambient: 
DirectionalLight {
    intensity: 0.0,                          // no sun beam
    ambient: Vec3::new(0.18, 0.20, 0.26),    // cold lifted fill
    ..DirectionalLight::default()
}
This is the “birch → clinical” move from the playground: a cold, lifted ambient pushes shadows toward cold fill rather than black, reading as a hostile over-lit lab — without flattening the raking hero spot that still carves the normal relief. If no DirectionalLight exists at all, a placeholder supplies a flat grey ambient (0.3) so geometry is at least visible.

9b · SpotLight — the workhorse of indoor mood

Positioned. Authoring contract: spawn with both a Transform and a SpotLight. Position is Transform.translation; aim is Transform.rotation · -Z (shines along local −Z). Build it with degree-valued cone half-angles via SpotLight::new:

SpotLight::new(color, intensity, range, inner_deg, outer_deg)
    .with_god_ray_intensity(1.4)   // optional, default 1.0
ParamDefaultWhat it does
color(1,1,1)Beam tint. Cool fluorescent (0.92,0.96,1.0) for clinical; warm (1.0,0.96,0.9) for comfort.
intensity1.0Goes high — playground hero spot is 28. Inverse-square falloff eats it (see §10).
range10 mSoft cutoff radius; also the shadow far-plane. Set ~2× the room height.
inner_deg20°Half-angle of the full-strength core.
outer_deg30°Half-angle where the beam reaches zero. Inner→outer is a smoothstep soft edge.
god_ray_intensity1.0Per-spot multiplier on fog scattering. 1.8 = emphatic beam, 0.4 = subtle, 0 = off. See §11.

Cone shape intuition. A tight inner/outer gap (e.g. 20°/22°) gives a hard-edged spotlight circle; a wide gap (42°/60°, the playground hero) gives a soft, equipment-like pool. Inner = outer makes a crisp cutoff with no falloff band.

The playground’s “reads as equipment, not cinematography” hero lamp:

SpotLight::new(Vec3::new(0.92,0.96,1.0), 28.0, range, 42.0, 60.0)
    .with_god_ray_intensity(1.4)
// mounted high, offset to a corner, aimed near-straight-down
// → rakes the floor, long hard shadows, carves the normal relief

9c · PointLight — omnidirectional accents

Positioned, pairs with a Transform (only translation used). No cone, no shadow support. Inverse-square with a soft range cutoff. Kinesis uses these for the dim red service-corridor accents:

// deep red, intensity 6, reach = tunnel length
ParamDefaultNotes
color(1,1,1)Unit tint.

10 · Why intensity goes so high — the attenuation model

Spots and points use a windowed inverse-square falloff (the UE4/Frostbite shape):

window = max(1 - (d/range)⁴, 0)
atten  = window² / (1 + d²)

The 1/(1+d²) is physically-motivated falloff with a guard against the blow-up at the lamp itself; the windowing term tapers smoothly to exactly zero at range (no hard edge). The practical consequence: brightness drops to ~1/(1+d²) with distance, so at 5 m a light is already at ~1/26 of source. That’s why the hero spot needs intensity 28 to read across a 5 m chamber, and the red point needs 6. If a light looks too dim, raise intensity before anything else.

11 · Shadows

Opt a spot into shadow-casting by attaching the Shadowcaster marker component (the playground’s spawn_spot(.., casts_shadow: true) does this). Cost is paid only by marked lights — both an extra depth pass and the forward shader’s comparison sample.

Softness — the F1 PCF cycle

Shadow edges are softened with PCF (percentage-closer filtering). F1 cycles the kernel; the shipping default is Soft3x3:

KernelTapsLook
Single1×1Hard edges. A baseline to see what PCF adds.
Soft3x3 (default)3×3Natural soft silhouette at 1024² indoor scale. Ship this.
Wide5x55×5Wider blur; exists for the A/B comparison, not as a recommendation.

Acne is handled by normal-offset bias (Sylvan/Holbert) — the sample position is pushed along the surface normal scaled by (1−n·l), plus a small slope-scaled rasterizer bias as far-plane defense. This is the modern recipe; you shouldn’t need to touch it.

Authoring a good shadow: mount the caster spot high and off-centre so it rakes — long shadows read as drama and reveal the normal relief. A near-straight-down lamp gives short, dull shadows. The hero lamp is offset to a corner for exactly this reason.

12 · Fog & god-rays — the atmosphere resource

Fog is a single World resource (one medium per scene), seeded off (density = 0). It does two jobs from five fields: height fog (extinction) and god-rays (in-scattering through spot cones).

FieldOff / rangeWhat it does
colorlinear RGB“What the room dissolves into” at infinity. Cool grey for cold dread, warm for dusk.
density0 = off; 0.03 thin → 0.2 thickThickness at base_height. 0 short-circuits the whole system.
base_heightworld yThe y where density = density; above it density falls off.
falloff0 = uniform; 0.3–0.5 typicalExponential height falloff (1/units). Higher = fog hugs the floor, thins overhead.
scattering0 = no rays; 0.2–0.6Single-scatter coefficient → god-ray brightness. Decoupled from density on purpose.

Why height fog, not flat fog

Constant-density fog reads as “dirty lens” — looking up looks like looking forward. Exponential height fog gives the floor a denser layer that thins overhead: the Gothic/Witcher mood. The shader integrates the optical depth along the view ray analytically (Wenzel 2007, GPU Gems 2), so it’s one closed-form evaluation per fragment, no raymarch.

God-rays — decoupled scattering

Physically, doubling density doubles both extinction and scattering. Games split them because authors want “thick haze, subtle glow” and “thin air, bright shafts” as independent moods — so scattering is its own knob. For each spot and each fragment, the shader analytically finds the segment of the view ray inside the cone (ray-cone intersection, Eberly), evaluates the in-scatter at the segment midpoint (Toth 2009), and adds it. Then each spot scales it by its own god_ray_intensity.

God-rays need three things on at once: density > 0 (a medium to scatter in), scattering > 0 (the medium’s coefficient), and a spot with god_ray_intensity > 0. Turn off any one and the beam vanishes. There is no volumetric shadow occlusion yet — a wall in front clips the ray (via the visible-surface distance) but the wall doesn’t carve dust-motes out of the beam. That’s a Game 2A+ upgrade if it earns its keep.

The playground’s cold thin haze + emphatic hero beam:

Fog {
    color: Vec3::new(0.05, 0.06, 0.08),  // cold near-black
    base_height: 0.0,
    density: 0.03,                        // thin
    falloff: 0.3,
    scattering: 0.25,
}
// + hero spot .with_god_ray_intensity(1.4)

F4 cycles fog presets Off → Light → Medium → Heavy for quick A/B.

13 · Tonemap & color grade — the zone’s color

The tonemap (fixed)

The HDR frame is compressed by a Narkowicz 2015 fitted ACES curve — the 5-MAD approximation everyone ships. It gives the soft highlight shoulder, gentle shadow toe and slight saturation push that read as “filmic.” You don’t configure it; you author into it (push emissive/lights past 1 and the shoulder rolls them off gracefully). Alternatives noted in the source if the look ever needs them: Hill ACES (closer chroma), Hable (warmer mids), Reinhard (cheap, muddy).

The grade — ASC CDL (lift / gamma / gain)

ColorGrade is a World resource applied post-tonemap, so your numbers map to what you see on screen, not to HDR scene-linear. The formula is the industry-standard ASC CDL primary:

graded = (color · gain + lift) ^ (1 / gamma)
FieldIdentityEffect (per channel)
gain(1,1,1)Highlight multiplier (shoulder tint). >1 brightens that channel’s highs.
lift(0,0,0)Shadow shift (toe tint). Added after gain.
gamma(1,1,1)Midtone reshape. >1 brightens mids, <1 darkens. Must stay > 0 (zero/negative → NaN).

Because it’s real ASC CDL in fixed order, a colorist can paste these exact numbers into DaVinci and see the same look. That portability is deliberate.

Shipped presets

lift (0, 0, 0.01) gamma (1, 1, 1) gain (1, 1, 1.02)

Hostile interrogation room — aggressive cold push; red pulled down, blue lifted. Pairs with cold ambient.

“Comfortable” cage — slight amber across shadows/mids/highs. The comfort side of the attitude axis.

F2 cycles Default → ChamberWhite → CageWarm → ServiceRed. CLINICAL_COLD exists in code but isn’t in the F2 cycle — set it directly on the resource (world.insert_resource(ColorGrade::CLINICAL_COLD)) like the scene build does.

Grade vs ambient — don’t confuse them. Ambient color (on the DirectionalLight) tints the actual fill light reaching surfaces and interacts with albedo and shadow. ColorGrade tints the finished image uniformly. For a cold room you usually want both: cold ambient so shadows read cold in 3D, plus a cold grade so the whole frame agrees.

13b · Highlight bloom — the faint glow

An optional, faint-by-default glow around the over-bright parts of the frame. Built as a half-res bright-pass pyramid (the modern dual-filter technique from Call of Duty: Advanced Warfare, 2014) but tuned for the 2005–2009 look it references — Half-Life 2, Witcher 1 — so it reads as a wide, soft, warm halation, not a tight modern lens flare. It composites into the HDR image before the tonemap (step C0), so the glow rolls through the ACES shoulder like any other highlight.

What blooms is decided by brightness, not by object. The bright-pass keeps only pixels whose HDR radiance exceeds threshold. Nothing is a "bloom source" by identity — a lamp whose surface isn’t bright enough won’t glow, and an emissive gel pushed past 1.0 will. To make something glow more, make it brighter: raise its emissive_intensity (or a light’s intensity) so it pushes further past the threshold. That’s exactly how the era authored it — there was no per-object bloom mask, you just made the thing bright.
FieldRangeEffect
enabledboolMaster gate. Off skips the whole pyramid build (zero GPU cost) and contributes nothing.
strength~0.0 – 0.5The master "how much glow." Additive, so values are small: 0.04 = faint highlight lift, 0.4+ = everything glows.
threshold~0.5 – 1.5HDR cutoff. ≥ 1.0 blooms only genuinely over-bright pixels; below 1.0 starts hazing midtones (the aggressive end).
knee0 – 1Soft ramp into the threshold, as a fraction of it. Stops surfaces popping in/out of bloom as they brighten.
filter_radius1.0 – 3.0Blur spread in source texels. 1.0 = tight/crisp, 2–3 = the wide soft era halo.

Presets: OFF · FAINT (strength 0.04, threshold 1.1 — the restrained shipping default) · ERA_GLOW (strength 0.15, threshold 0.9, wider + warmer — pronounced HL2/Witcher halation) · EVERYTHING_GLOWS (strength 0.45, threshold 0.5 — the far end of the dial, a deliberate white-out for one scene). F7 cycles Off → Faint → EraGlow → EverythingGlows.

Bloom on a translucent surface. An emissive AlphaBlend surface (like the food gel) reaches the framebuffer as emissive · opacity, so opacity and emissive_intensity jointly set how hard it blooms. The gel at opacity 0.8 × emissive_intensity 2.5 peaks around 1.9 — comfortably over ERA_GLOW’s 0.9 threshold.

14 · Vignette

Radial corner darkening + optional tint, applied after the grade. A separate resource from ColorGrade on purpose: the grade is the look (colorist territory, film-portable); the vignette is a compositor mood tool you modulate independently — heavier in tense moments, off in calm ones — without disturbing the zone’s color.

FieldRangeEffect

Presets: CINEMATIC (0.4, inner 0.5, outer 1.0) — “this is a photographed image,” the calm default · OPPRESSIVE (0.9, inner 0.2, outer 0.9) — corners crush to black, tunnel-vision, reads as threat. The vignette is elliptical (tracks aspect ratio) like every other engine’s. F3 cycles Default → Cinematic → Oppressive.

15 · Post overlay — the full-screen compositor slot

The last thing composited, on top of the graded + vignetted frame. A PostOverlay resource binds a full-screen texture and composites it one of three ways. It’s the slot for screen-space narrative effects.

BlendMathIntended use
AlphaBlendmix(scene, tex.rgb, tex.a · intensity)Death red-noise — the texture’s alpha masks where it lands.
Multiplymix(scene, scene·tex.rgb, intensity)Hunger warm-tint — modulates the whole frame.
Additivescene + tex.rgb · intensityFlashes — instrument glow, lightning; allowed past white before the final clamp.

intensity = 0 (default) is the off switch — a no-op regardless of bound texture or blend. The texture is the game’s to supply (the engine ships none); it loads through the same registry as material textures, so it’s F5-reloadable. F6 cycles the intensity+blend for A/B (it leaves your texture handle alone).

18 · How the knobs interact (the gotchas)

  • Ambient lives on the DirectionalLight, and is read even at intensity 0. To light an indoor room with “fill,” spawn a zero-intensity sun carrying ambient.
  • Lights need high intensity because of 1/(1+d²) falloff. Dim ≠ broken — raise intensity first.
  • Emissive ignores shadows and ambient (it’s the surface’s own light) but does dim through fog (fog mixes before tonemap). A lamp behind haze reads as a haze-coloured smudge.
  • Fog gates god-rays. No density → no rays, whatever scattering or god_ray_intensity say.
  • Grade is post-tonemap, fog is pre-tonemap. Cold grade + warm fog will fight; match them.
  • opacity only matters under AlphaBlend. An Opaque material with opacity 0.2 still renders solid (REPLACE blend ignores alpha).
  • fresnel is a combo, not a switch. It needs low opacity + low roughness + AlphaBlend to read as glass; alone it just lifts edge specular.
  • normal_strength wants to be low (0.3–0.6). 1.0 reads as a bump demo. The look is subtle relief + hard raking light.
  • triplanar reinterprets uv_scale.x as world repeats/metre — the other UV fields are ignored in that mode.
  • Color textures sRGB, normal maps linear. Wrong path = silently wrong shading.
  • Only spots cast shadows, max 4, and only with the Shadowcaster marker. Points never shadow.
  • Raking beats overhead. A near-vertical lamp gives dull short shadows and flat relief; offset + angle it to rake.
