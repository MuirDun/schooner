# Act 3 — Flight

**Purpose:** Teach repulsion / flight. Introduce the Child as a watching presence. Establish chamber-state persistence. Show evidence of previous test subjects (who died for real). Begin offering unintended solutions.

**Length:** Five chambers. Approximately 50–70 minutes. The longest act.

**Lighting:** Slightly dimmer white than Act 1, modulated by Researcher attitude (see [`../systems.md`](../systems.md) §Feedback channels). The Researcher has noted the player's discomfort with raw spotlights and adjusted.

**Audio signature:** The industrial hum is the same. Mahli vocalizations more frequent, with a clear second voice — higher, more variable, more curious. This is the Child, though never named.

**Hunger:** Variable, moderate-to-severe.

---

## Room 6 — Flight introduction

A large vertical chamber. A high ledge holds the food. No stackable blocks are present. The chamber demands the new ability.

A glyph overlay appears for repulsion (mouse-silhouette with the "3" button highlighted and a push-away-from-surface arrow). When the player aims at a wall and engages repulsion, they push themselves off it.

The player learns to repulse against walls and ceilings to gain height. Death by falling becomes possible and likely.

**First persistent chamber state.** When the player dies in this room, the chamber does not reset. Their previously-positioned objects remain where they were. *No body is left.* The player wakes near the entrance.

Most players will notice the room state has not been tidied and feel a chill. **The Researcher has stopped resetting the room.**

## Room 7 — The Child appears

A more complex flight chamber requiring chained repulsions and timing.

In the window: two eyes. The Researcher's, large and slow. And smaller — closer to the glass — a different eye, jade-iris, more intricate pupil pattern, moving and blinking.

The Child watches the player. The Child's eye dilates when the player makes interesting moves. It drifts away when the player is stuck for too long. **This is observation, not scoring** — the Child's attitude does not update from chamber play.

This is the player's introduction to the Child. The two-eye composition tells the player there are now two observers, and they are different.

## Room 8 — The Hidden Room (mandatory beat)

A standard flight chamber with one secret: a small hole in a side wall, at a height that requires repulsion to reach. The hole is large enough for the player to enter.

Inside the hole: a small dead-end space. Three or four desiccated human bodies, posed as if they fell asleep. **Drawings cover every surface.**

**These are subjects who died for real.** The player, who is reincarnated, is unique in this place. The drawings are the accumulated testimony of every subject who came before and ran out. **This is the only place in the chambers where wall art exists**, and the contrast with the sterile chambers around it is the point.

The drawings are extensive. Stick figures. Diagrams of chambers. Maps. A drawing of a giant tentacle with an arrow pointing away from it. A drawing of two figures embracing — perhaps friends. A drawing of a figure on its knees, head bowed, with what looks like a tear.

The player can leave whenever they want. There is no puzzle here. **There is only the past.**

When the player exits and continues, the lighting feels slightly different. The windows feel slightly more present. The hum feels slightly more oppressive. Nothing has been said. Everything has changed.

**Note for level design:** This room must be findable but not unmissable. Players who explore find it. Players who rush past it have a different experience of the rest of the game — less informed, more isolated. Both are valid playthroughs.

## Room 9 — Unintended solutions emerge

A demanding flight chamber with a clear intended solution (repulsion through a sequence of platforms). It also has, hidden but discoverable, a destructible wall that bypasses most of the puzzle.

A player who finds the destructible wall and breaks through it skips most of the chamber.

- **First unintended solution across the playthrough:** The Researcher's eye in the window shifts to the **curious** state — pupil dilates, iris brightens, eye leans toward the glass. (Researcher attitude: +.) The Child's eye dilates as well, but its attitude does not update.
- **Second and subsequent unintended solutions across the playthrough:** The Researcher's eye shifts toward **annoyed** — iris dims, pupil contracts, eye fixes. (Researcher attitude: −.) The chamber after a second unintended solution shows the first comfort shift toward neglect (slightly more pitting, slightly cooler light) on next entry.

This is the parasite-path teaching layer. The game is showing the player that unintended solutions exist and that the Researcher *cares* about them — first as anomalies, then as contamination. **The Researcher never communicates this directly.** The eye and the room are the only channels. See [`../themes.md`](../themes.md).

## Room 10 — Climax of the flight act

A long, vertical chamber requiring the integration of all learned mechanics: hands, telekinesis, throwing, repulsion. Persistent chamber state means accumulated displaced objects across the player's attempts.

This is where many players will die five to ten times. The chamber respects the persistence rule strictly: every previous attempt's debris remains placed where the player left it.

In the window: both eyes. The Child's eye is *very* close to the glass during this chamber, watching with apparent fascination. The Researcher's eye is further, slower — current state depending on attitude.

The chamber ends with another tunnel. Lights flicker. Player faints.
