# Endings

There is no Act 6. All endings flow out of Act 5.

---

## Ending routing

| Condition | Ending |
|---|---|
| Death threshold exceeded at any prior point | **C** (the only true death) |
| Chose the gap at the Escape fork | **B** (Parasite) |
| Reached intended exit OR walked to cage at fork — Child attitude **high** | **A1** (Pet) |
| Reached intended exit OR walked to cage at fork — Child not high, Researcher attitude **acceptable** | **A2** (Holding) |
| Reached intended exit OR walked to cage at fork — Child not high, Researcher attitude **poor** | **A3** (Sorter) |

**Note:** Child attitude is dominant for the A1 route. If the Child has bonded with the player, the Child claims the pet, regardless of how the Researcher feels about the experiment outcome. The Researcher's regard then conditions only how *present* the Researcher is in the A1 epilogue (see A1 below).

---

## Ending C — Discard

Not a playable sequence. The player simply dies one final time and does not wake up. A black screen. A single Mahli vocalization, brief and disinterested. Then **Epilogue C**.

This is the only true death in the game. There is no reincarnation. **The Researcher has decided the subject is not worth reviving.**

## Ending A1 — Prodigal Son (Child high)

The cage has been replaced with a beautifully appointed habitat. Soft bedding, a small enclosed sleeping area, toys including the instrument from Act 4, a window with a view of dim red light. The Child's eye is at the glass, dilated and excited. The Child has won a pet. The Child is happy. The player is alive and cared for and cannot leave. Routes to **Epilogue A1**.

**Researcher presence in A1 (depends on Researcher attitude):**

- **Good Researcher attitude:** The Researcher occasionally visits — a slow tentacle gently re-arranging a toy, the larger topaz eye appearing at the glass for a few seconds. The Researcher approves of the outcome.
- **Neutral Researcher attitude:** The Researcher's eye appears sometimes, briefly, dispassionate. Tentacle visits do not occur.
- **Low Researcher attitude:** The Researcher's eye never appears in A1. The Child got their pet. The Researcher walked away from the project. The pet enclosure has all the warmth of the Child, and none of the parent's attention. This reads to the player as a subtle bittersweet — the Child got what they wanted, but the experiment ended in disappointment.

## Ending A2 — Holding Pen (Child not high, Researcher acceptable)

The cage is gone. The player wakes in a small (10x10m) enclosure with bare walls, gravel floor, a few metal blocks. Red ambient light. Other identical enclosures are visible through gaps in the wall — many of them, occupied or empty. The player is alive, fed on schedule, and indistinguishable from the other vermin. Routes to **Epilogue A2**.

## Ending A3 — Sorter (Child not high, Researcher poor)

The player wakes in a small storage room with bright spotlights and four holes in the walls. Boxes of different colors begin entering through one hole. The player must sort them into the matching colored holes. Every five correct sorts, a small food brick is dispensed. The player is alive and useful. Routes to **Epilogue A3**.

## Ending B — Parasite

Triggered by taking the gap at the escape fork. The player slips into the seams of the Mahli world and is not pursued. Routes to **Epilogue B**.

## Epilogues

Each non-C epilogue is a short, looping, almost-passive play space rather than a cutscene. The player can move and look around. The menu's "Exit" option is replaced by the text *"оставить его тут..." / "leave him here..."*. **The player chooses when to stop watching.**

### Epilogue A1 — *You became a pet*

The pet enclosure. Soft bedding. Toys. The instrument. Red dimmer light. Tentacles periodically reach in: dropping a ball, a new toy, food. The Child's eye visits often, sometimes excited, sometimes calm.

The player can play with the toys forever. They can play the instrument — and they will hear the same beautiful sound from Act 4, but the Child's eye no longer reacts the way it once did. **The music that earned the bond is now decoration.** This is the one and only re-use of music in the game; it is the act's quiet melancholy.

There is no exit from the enclosure. The hunger system is **off** — food arrives reliably. The player is safe and loved and trapped.

End text: ***Вы стали питомцем... / You became a pet...***

### Epilogue A2 — *You were placed in general holding*

The 10x10m holding cell. Red dim, **permanent and unchanging** — the only constant the player has. Distant tentacles pass occasionally in the corridor visible through the gap in the wall, but they are **not permanent** — they appear and disappear. Other cells visible through gaps, some occupied by figures who do not move. Food drops on a schedule that the player cannot influence.

**The verb is waiting.** The player can move, look around, push blocks, jump — but nothing meaningful happens. Stillness produces no consequence; activity produces no consequence. Waiting becomes life. This is the bleakest ending in the game and is intentionally absent of a mini-game or task. The bleakness is the absence of a verb.

The hunger system is off — food arrives on its schedule. The player will not starve. They will simply wait.

End text: ***Вас отсадили в общие отсеки до следующей надобности... / You were moved to general holding until the next need...***

### Epilogue A3 — *You became a tool*

The sorting room. Bright spotlights. Boxes arrive. The player sorts them. Food bricks dispense after every five correct sorts. The player can stop sorting; nothing prevents this except hunger.

The hunger system is **active** in this epilogue — stop sorting and you starve. This is the only non-C epilogue with active hunger pressure; it is what defines being *useful* as opposed to being *kept* or being *hidden*.

End text: ***Вы стали инструментом... / You became a tool...***

### Epilogue B — *You became a parasite*

A network of gaps, vents, crevices. Red oppressive light everywhere. The player can move freely through this network but it leads only to other parts of itself. Food is scattered through it — the player must search constantly. Hunger is always pressing in this epilogue. The player's previous parasite-hideout from the escape sequence is visible — a slightly more developed version, now their home. They can return to it.

The world is huge and the player is hidden in its seams. No one is looking for them. No one will ever look for them. **They are free in the sense that no one wants them.**

End text: ***Вы стали паразитом... / You became a parasite...***

### Epilogue C — *Discarded*

A waste chute or industrial bin, dimly lit red. The player wakes on a heap of refuse. Other bodies — desiccated, anonymous — surround them. They cannot stand. The screen darkens slowly. **They die for real this time** — no injection, no revival. The only true death in the game.

End text: ***Безнадёжный. / Hopeless.***

## Epilogue note on choosing return at the fork

If the player took the escape fork toward their own cage instead of the gap, the epilogue text is modified. After the ending text ("You became a pet" / "You were moved to general holding" / "You became a tool"), an additional line appears, faintly:

***Вы могли уйти. / You could have left.***

This single line of additional text appears nowhere else in the game and exists to acknowledge the player's choice. **It is the only direct address to the player anywhere in the script.** Use it sparingly — it is the only one.
