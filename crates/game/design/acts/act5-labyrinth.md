# Act 5 — The Labyrinth

**Purpose:** Final exam. Integrate all mechanics. Demonstrate that the Child is gone — the experiment is concluding. Contain both endings within a single act: the intended exit and the escape branch.

**Length:** Approximately 30–50 minutes. Longer than the previous chamber acts because Act 5 contains all endgame content.

**Lighting:** Cooler than previous chambers, modulated by Researcher attitude. The white spotlights are dimmer. Some sections of the labyrinth are nearly dark. The Researcher cares less about visibility now.

**Audio signature:** Quieter than the chambers. The hum is more present, less competed-with by Mahli vocalizations. **The Child's voice is absent.** The Researcher's voice is heard once or twice, distant and procedural.

**Hunger:** Severe.

---

## The Child's absence

The Child's jade eye does not appear in any window during Act 5. The Child has been removed from the observation deck. **This absence is the act's emotional signature.** The player who became attached to being watched is now alone with the Researcher.

## The space

A single connected labyrinth, approximately 5–10 minutes of straight-line traversal but laid out to require 20–30 minutes of exploration and puzzle-solving. Multiple paths. Dead ends. Areas requiring different mechanics in combination.

The labyrinth is not maze-like in the disorienting sense. It is a layered space with vertical and horizontal complexity, designed to test the player's mastery of all four verbs (push/pull, throw, repulsion-flight, telekinesis at range).

Chamber state persists. Displaced objects remain. The player's deaths leave traces in the form of placed/displaced objects but no body.

**No wall art anywhere in the labyrinth.** The labyrinth was built fresh, no previous occupants lived here. The sterility is the loneliness. See [`../themes.md`](../themes.md).

**No Mahli warning of any kind.** If the player has been finding unintended solutions, the Researcher's displeasure is communicated through the now-degraded chamber comfort (cool light, pitted iron) and the annoyed eye-state in any windows. **The Mahli do not draw signs for the player.** The eye and the room are the only channels.

---

## The two outcomes — both inside Act 5

Act 5 culminates in one of two outcomes. There is **no Act 6**. Endings flow directly out of Act 5.

### Outcome 1 — The intended exit

At the labyrinth's far end, a final clear exit. The player who reaches it without breaking through any unintended walls completes the labyrinth as designed.

Reaching this exit triggers a brief transition (lights flicker, vision desaturates, faint) and the player wakes in one of three ending spaces, determined by attitude. See [`endings.md`](endings.md) for A1 / A2 / A3.

### Outcome 2 — Escape (a continuation of Act 5)

In several locations within the labyrinth, destructible walls or apparent gaps exist that lead to service spaces — the back of the world. These are the parasite-path entries.

A player who breaks through one of these walls enters the **Escape sequence**, which is a continuation of Act 5, not a separate act.

The destructible walls are findable but not obvious. They look slightly different from solid walls — small cracks, oxidation patterns, hairline seams. A player who has been paying attention to wall details will spot them. A player rushing through will miss them.

### Escape sequence (15–20 minutes, contained within Act 5)

1. **Transition.** A sudden shift from the white-lit clinical labyrinth to a vast, dim, red-lit industrial space. The player emerges high on a wall or ceiling area, looking down into a massive Mahli room. They can see the labyrinth from above, glowing through its top — it is a small thing in a much larger space. A Mahli silhouette (the Researcher) is visible at the labyrinth's edge, scanning it with eyes the size of cars. The player must move silently along outer surfaces, behind pipes, between iron layers.

2. **The previous occupant's hideout.** The player finds a small alcove with signs of recent habitation — a primitive nest, a partially burned remnant of dense Mahli paper, a few scratched marks on the iron beside it. A previous escapee was here. They are not here now. The player can examine the space and continue. **This is the only place in Act 5 where wall art appears**, and it consists of a few scratched marks only — not the elaborate testimony of the Act 3 hidden room.

3. **The food storage.** The player enters a Mahli food storage area. Many bricks of food, of various qualities. The player can eat, fill their belly, and gather more to carry. The act of gathering takes time and exposes the player. Mid-gather, a Mahli enters the storage to retrieve something — the player must hide behind crates or pipes as enormous tentacles probe the space inches from them. **This is the only moment of true tension in the game** — a stealth sequence. No combat. Pure avoidance.

4. **The fork.** Past the storage, the player reaches a junction. To one side: a narrow gap leading deeper into the iron walls — a perfect parasite hideout. To the other side: a route that loops back around and ends with a view of the player's *own* cage from outside. The cage is in whichever state the attitude system rendered it for Act 4 — if it was made cozy, the player sees their warm pet-room from outside, the Child's eye still watching the empty space, waiting for them.

This is the player's choice. **Two paths, no labels. They walk into one.**

- **Take the gap:** Routes to **Ending B (Parasite).**
- **Walk to the cage:** The player is captured (or willingly returns) and routes to the appropriate **Ending A** flavor (A1 / A2 / A3) based on accumulated attitude. The epilogue is modified — see [`endings.md`](endings.md) §Epilogue note on choosing return.

**The "walk back to the cage" choice is the most thematically loaded option in the game.** A player who escaped to find freedom and then chose to return understands something they didn't before.
