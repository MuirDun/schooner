# Themes — The spine of Kinesis

This document is the canonical reference for what Kinesis is *about*. Every other design decision must answer to it. If a scene, system, or asset pulls against this spine, the spine wins.

---

## The mainline

> Kinesis is a retrospective on the use of animals as instruments for our interests. *How does it feel from the other side of the cage.*

This is the question the work asks. The work does not answer it. The player answers it with their body, over four hours, and lives with the answer the epilogue gives them.

---

## The five load-bearing positions

### 1. The player is not the point of the experiment

The cybernetic implant is. The Mahli are studying their own technology — its tolerances, its behaviors, its failure modes. The player is the substrate the technology lives in.

The player will spend the game assuming the game is about them — their hunger, their bond with the Child, their escape. **The Mahli think the game is about the apparatus.** The gap between those two readings is the game.

### 2. The Researchers are not cruel

Indifference, not malice. A scientist studying a lab animal does not hate it; they do not love it; they manage it for outcomes. There is no scene in which a Mahli enjoys the player's suffering. There is no scene in which a Mahli regrets it. There is no moral arc on the Mahli side, because there is no moral relationship across the gap.

The Researcher's attitude system reflects this: regard, not affection. **Curiosity is a scientific reaction. Annoyance is a procedural reaction.** Both are professional.

### 3. The chimp-human gap is irreducible

The cultural and cognitive distance between humans and Mahli is the chimpanzee-to-human gap. Communication is impossible across it, and the Mahli know this and do not try.

**Concretely, this forbids:**

- Any Mahli writing, pictogram, or symbol presented in the game world for the player to read.
- Any moment of mutual understanding, decoded language, or shared meaning.
- Any "tutorial" framing in which the Mahli teach the player something on purpose.
- Any climactic recognition. The Child never realizes the player is intelligent. The Researcher never wonders.

The Child's reactions are response-to-stimulus, the way a child watching a hamster laughs when the hamster runs in its wheel. The Researcher's environmental adjustments are *how they manage their lab*, not *how they speak to the subject*. **There is no intellectual communication anywhere in the game.**

### 4. Loneliness is a core line

Humans exist on this planet in large numbers — vermin in Mahli complexes, biologically adapted to sulfur-rich waste. But in this facility they are only two things: **test subjects, or wild intruders.** The rat-lab framing: cockroaches in a biology lab are either being studied or scuttling under the door.

The player is the only persistent subject. Every other human the player will ever see is dead, captured, or a brief silhouette in the dark.

Wall art exists only in places where humans *lived* — the Act 3 hidden room, the Act 5 escape hideout, the inside of the cage canopy. **Nowhere else.** The chambers and labyrinth are sterile. *That sterility is the point.* The player should feel the absence of fellow-feeling more often than they feel its presence.

### 5. The Mahli do not communicate, even to teach

The player learns the verbs through their own body and the world. The HUD glyph overlays are *the player's perception of their own apparatus*, not a Mahli teaching device. The Mahli set up the chambers to *produce data*, not to *instruct* the subject; the player's discovery of the verbs is incidental to the experiment's design from the Mahli side.

This is a fine distinction but a load-bearing one. The chambers contain affordances. The Mahli watch what the substrate does with them. The substrate, by emerging into language we read as understanding, has crossed the experimental threshold — but the Mahli do not register this crossing.

## What this means for player experience

The player should feel, in roughly this order:

1. **Hunger as the first language.** Before anything else, they learn to want.
2. **Being watched.** The eye is the constant. Behavior produces no response in real time, only over long arcs.
3. **The discovery that they are not alone — and that being-not-alone is worse than being alone.** The Act 3 hidden room and the wall scratches under the canopy. Others were here. None of them are still here.
4. **The first warmth.** The Child's gift, the instrument, the touch. The Child finds the player charming. The player has no idea what kind of attention this is.
5. **The withdrawal of warmth.** Act 5. The Child is gone. The Researcher's care has rationed. The player is alone with the apparatus that was always the point.
6. **The choice without labels.** Escape, return, or be sorted into one of three futures, none of which is freedom.

## Anti-patterns (things to resist)

- **The Mahli warning the player.** Cut on sight. Any pictogram, symbol, or arranged-world-message intended for the player to interpret is a thesis violation. The Researcher's annoyance is communicated through chamber comfort and eye state — never through composed messages.
- **Human wall art outside hideouts.** Drawings imply someone had time and surface to draw. Test subjects in chambers had neither. Drawings live only where humans lived.
- **"You are the chosen one."** The player is durable, persistent, unique among subjects. None of this matters to the Mahli. None of this should ever be framed by the script as *meaningful* in a heroic sense.
- **Companionship.** Resist the urge to give the player a friend. Other subjects are dead. The Child is not a friend, even when warm. The work is lonelier than any reflex wants to make it.
- **Closure on the gap.** The Mahli never understand the player. The player never understands the Mahli. No third party translates. The work ends with the gap intact.

## Where the spine meets the systems

- **Hunger curve as tempo, not pressure** — supports the substrate framing. The Mahli ration interest; hunger is one expression of how much they care today.
- **Eye-state convention** — the most important feedback channel. Indifferent / curious / annoyed are *scientific* states, not emotional ones.
- **Comfort signals never give mechanical advantage** — regard is regard, not handicap. The room is nicer or worse; the puzzle is the same.
- **The instrument as the only music** — the player's first and only act of expression. The Child reacts. The Mahli do not score it. The fact that this scene happens at all is the warmth-trap.
- **The single direct address: *"You could have left."*** — the only time the work breaks frame, used once, to acknowledge that one specific choice was a choice. Never used elsewhere.
