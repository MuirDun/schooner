# The Game:

This should be a small game. You could even call it a demo, but still feel like a full-fledged game. It should take about 3-4 hours to complete. Like the first Portal, you could say.

There's a save between each room and act. It's saved as a new one. There are no manual saves—only autosaves between levels.

# The World:

Somehow, we ended up on an alien planet as humans. We know nothing and remember nothing about ourselves. We weren't abducted—the humans here are like cockroaches and rodents living in the rooms where the aliens live. We'll call them "Mahli."

This planet is a volcanic, acidic giant, with views like Io (a moon of Jupiter) and an atmosphere like Venus. People here can only live in the Makhli complexes—giant iron complexes (of the kind common on Io). People live in the Makhli complexes like cockroaches, subsisting on organic waste containing sulfur and the like. The Makhli themselves eat (we have to figure it out), and people who have lived on this planet for millennia have adapted to such a diet. It's unclear how exactly humans originally arrived here, but the Makhli aren't a super-advanced race—their technology is generally not super-advanced compared to our modern ones, like cyberpunk, high cybernetic technology, but not a galactic power. The Makhli are supergiants relative to us; we're about the size of a Djungarian hamster relative to a human, or even smaller. They're enormous, black, octopus-whale-like creatures, moving on tentacles. They don't require bright light; indoors, they use red lamps. But for the people in the research chambers, they install white spotlights so they can see what they're doing. They have black eyes with a topaz sheen and intricate pupils—their eyes, their general silhouette, and parts of their tentacles are the only things the player will see.

Originally, the Manhi lived in the planet's underground caves, where various cave plants grow that convert carbon dioxide into oxygen. The Manhi themselves live on oxygen and digest sulfur. They are semi-aquatic creatures that live in canyons and underground lakes. The planet's surface is fatal to them.

We are test subjects in a biological research center. We are subjected to cybernetic experiments, particularly those related to kinetics. The apparatus inside us allows us to emit energy currents that allow us to move objects, push, and jump. Nature is such that we emit invisible, moving energy, like the directed force of magnetism. We remember nothing, understand nothing, and cannot speak. We are most likely a special kind of cloned human, particularly resistant to cybernetic experiments than "ordinary" humans living in tunnels and holes in metal. But this also makes us especially sensory-sensitive—we are still human, and like the player, we understand nothing of where we are or what awaits us—we know nothing of the world beyond the cage.

# Scenario:

## Act 1. Beginning

Begining, darkness. Flashes. Red noises, like pressure on the eyes. Clicking and humming sounds are heard in the background.

### Room 1
Then we wake up in a room. It's a cramped, stuffy space. It's all made of rough, rusty iron. The lighting is extremely bright, white. We feel severe pain in our stomachs and backs, and it's difficult to move. We see scattered, compressed blocks of sulfur, flecked with white. Everything tells us we should jump up these boxes, as a distinct, sharp smell of something bitter and vinegary is emanating from them (how should I put it? A glow? A cloud of gas?) (Narrative design is needed; we, like sweaty rats, must figure out for ourselves how and why to get somewhere). Jumping up, we finally reach a small block of gelatinous food (thus quenching both thirst and hunger). Having recharged, the pain in our stomachs subsided. Ahead of us is a hole and a black tunnel.

#### Mechanics:

- Left mouse button - push the object in front of us forward
- Right mouse button - pull towards you
- Both buttons held down - hold in your hands
- The hungrier you get, the food becomes brighter and more visible

### Room 2

After entering the tunnel, we came to the next room. It has a similar design, but the boxes are now out of reach—they're up high. The player needs a hint that pressing *2* activates the "telekinesis" skill.

#### Mechanics:
- Mode 1: Push-pull-hold with your hands
- Mode 2: Same mechanics as with hands, but now with telekinesis. Mouse wheel - forward/backward

### Room 3-8

Within these, we'll move forward in a linear fashion. The difficulty will increase. Different nuances will appear from room to room. In each room, there's a huge glass window on the right side, behind which lies complete darkness. Plot twist—all this time, a huge black eye stares at us from this window; it moves occasionally, but it's hard for the player to notice. There are patterns and white-topaz stripes on the pupil, along with small glares. In one of the rooms, the darkness inside the window will change. Now, huge red spotlights and various silhouettes of objects will be visible in the distance—this is the researcher's room; he's moved away. From the window.
If we die during the process (we need to think through the death effect), we are put back, and everything in the room returns to how it was. In reality, it's not quite death, but more like a fatal fainting spell/clinical death—we are revived with injections.

In the last room, after entering the tunnel, we find ourselves in a sealed space, and the door closes behind us. We faint.

## Act 2. Respite

We are locked in a cage-like aquarium. It's completely glass, with dirt and gravel underneath, and some rocks. It's about 30-30 meters in size. The room is scattered, and in one corner there's a canopy; we emerge not far from it. Nothing is visible beyond the walls of the aquarium—the bright light obscures everything, but we see red dots there and some awkwardly moving silhouettes. After about two minutes of wandering back and forth, the lid opens, and a giant tentacle tosses us an object that can be thrown by pressing the mouse wheel while holding it with both hands. We now see the same image from the window facing us again, and now we see it from a wide angle, realizing it's an eye, and it's pointed at us. After about five minutes, a hatch opens. We can enter it to continue the game. If we don't enter, our cage will tilt, and we'll roll in (moving toward the ending where we're thrown out).

#### New Mechanics

- **Throwing**: Hold down both mouse buttons and press the mouse wheel to throw an object forward.
- **Attitude**: How the Monkies treat us. Jumping, using your room, playing with objects, and dying in the room once all increase their attitude toward us. Complete disregard and inaction, the destruction of the room, and constant dying lowers

## Act 3. Flight

Here, things change a bit. We're immediately given an idea of ​​how to use the new ability—magnetism-powered flight. By pressing *3*, we select the "repulsion" mode. With this, we direct energy toward where we're looking, thus "pushing" ourselves away from that location. In other words, we direct magnetism toward ourselves. This allows us to climb inaccessible surfaces. The next five rooms will revolve around this. Dying in these rooms is much easier. Some levels will literally be impossible to complete in a single life. But now, the levels remain as they were before death. It's easy to die from a fall from a height. As we progress, we become increasingly aware of what's outside the window—Monkhee is watching us, studying us. The light has become slightly dimmer—it seems they've realized we don't like the super-bright lighting. One of the rooms closer to us is particularly creepy – it has a hole in the iron. Entering it reveals several dead bodies and drawings on the walls. Drawings can also be seen frequently on the walls in this act. Another, slightly smaller, eye pattern is sometimes visible in the window – this is Manhi's child, who is also watching us.

In different flight levels, you can find "unconventional" ways to complete the level. Completion results from finding a peculiar hole or dilapidated crevice, which can be widened by using "force" – throwing.

In this location, attitude changes towards us:

- Every 5th death – attitude decreases.
- If you complete the level unconventionally once, attitude increases. If you do it again, attitude decreases.

## Act 4. The Game

We return to the cage house again. Depending on our interactions, the landscape and the aquarium itself change – if positive, it becomes a little more cozy, and bedding and tasty food appear under the canopy. We notice that the very same eye pattern of the Manhi child is watching us through the aquarium window. It's roughly the same scenario as last time: the child throws us an incomprehensible object – a large black rectangle with holes the size of two or three people. When we interact with the object, "energizing one of the holes," music is played. We notice the eye begins to twitch, and at one point, after a long game, the child reaches out with a tentacle and strokes our head (at this point, we can try to escape; he'll try to reach it and then pull his hand away). Five minutes later, the same thing happens – the hatch opens.

## Act 5. Labyrinth

This is the last time we enter the chambers. Now it's not a series of closed rooms, but a single labyrinth. Using our abilities, we must find a way out. Death rolls us back, but what we did in the labyrinth remains. If we die n times in a row, the experiment ends with us being thrown into the trash (ending **C**).

In several places in the labyrinth, it's possible to find a clear way to "escape" by destroying a passage. Previously, at the beginning of the labyrinth, if we made even one non-standard passage during the flight act, we'll be informed by a picture that we are strictly forbidden to destroy anything. If we destroy the passage, we'll enter **Act 6. Escape**.

If we successfully complete the level (should take about 15 minutes), at the end we'll encounter the final puzzle: **Act 6. Judgment Day**
